Budget Is Not Authorization

While testing recovery behavior in my AI Quality Platform, I found a failure that looked harmless by every cost metric. The system remained under its spend limit and provider-call ceiling. It was still wrong.

The failure

I had intentionally authorized one replacement provider call after an earlier request ended in an ambiguous network state. The replacement completed successfully. Then the runner returned to the normal campaign loop and began another campaign arm—making a provider call I had never authorized.

The distinction

Quotas answer “How much can the system do?” Authorization answers “What is the system allowed to do?”

Those controls can overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A system can be perfectly within budget while acting outside the scope a human approved.

Fixing the orchestration boundary

The recovery call itself was constrained correctly. The failure happened afterward, when control fell back into the ordinary workflow. I separated recovery into its own isolated execution path: it can run the approved replacement, validate and score the result, and then it must stop.

01

Single-use authority

One approval authorizes exactly one replacement dispatch.

02

Short-lived scope

Authorization expires and can be revoked instead of becoming ambient permission.

03

Replay protection

A consumed approval cannot be reused for another provider call.

04

Isolated control flow

Recovery validates its result and stops without rejoining the campaign loop.

Designing systems that stop well

This project is less about whether an agent can finish a workflow than whether it recognizes when it should not. In another experiment, an underspecified divide-by-zero requirement remained ambiguous even after bounded escalation. The workflow stopped at INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE instead of inventing a product decision. Once the requirement was explicit, it progressed through requirements analysis, deterministic QA intake, adversarial verification, and stopped again at a human sign-off gate—as designed.

The platform today

AI Quality Platform is an experimental, governed multi-agent reliability system with provider-neutral model routing, a SQLite-backed runtime, default-deny authorization, structured validation, durable audit evidence, and opt-in real-provider testing. It has 101 passing offline tests and its first public release, while intentionally leaving real side-effect adapters disabled.
